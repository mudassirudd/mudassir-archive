Decisions and trade-offs

WooCommerce rather than a custom build

I could have built this as a custom application. I didn't, because the client's actual constraint wasn't the software — it was that they needed to change prices on a Sunday night without calling anyone. WooCommerce gives them an admin they already half understand, a payment ecosystem that's already been through compliance, and no dependency on me staying reachable.

The cost: plugin surface area and update maintenance, and less control over performance than I'd have with something I wrote. For this business that was clearly the right trade — a custom storefront they can't operate is a worse product than a conventional one they can.

A custom theme rather than a premium one

Premium themes are faster to stand up and slower to live with — they arrive with page builders and options panels you end up fighting. Building the theme meant the markup matched the catalogue structure rather than being bent around it.

The cost: more of my time up front, and no third-party support to fall back on.

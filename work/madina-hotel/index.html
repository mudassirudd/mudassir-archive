Madina Hotel — getting a landmark found online

Madina Hotel has been serving biryani near the Charminar since 1947. Ask anyone in the Old City and they'll know it. Search for it online and it barely showed up. I built the site, then took on the SEO, then the Google Business Profile, and now run the paid search — including the part I got badly wrong on day one.

The situation

This is an unusual kind of problem. Most businesses that can't be found online also can't be found offline — they have no reputation to convert. Madina Hotel has the opposite shape: seventy-odd years of reputation in the neighbourhood, and almost no presence in the place where people now decide where to eat.

Which means the job isn't building an audience. It's connecting an existing reputation to the search that already happens every evening — somebody within two kilometres typing "biryani near me" who would absolutely go if they knew it was there.

What I did

  • Built the website — the engagement started here, structured around local search intent rather than as a brochure.
  • Took on the on-page SEO, which I'd picked up doing earlier client work rather than being hired for.
  • Cleaned up the Google Business Profile. This is the unglamorous one and probably the highest-value: for "biryani near me", the map pack is what ranks, not the website. A neglected profile loses to a well-maintained one regardless of how good the site is.
  • Set up and now run Google Ads. See below.

On-page work is done. Off-page — citations, links, review flow — is what I'm moving onto next.

The ads mistake

Ads were the part I hadn't done before, and it showed.

Day one, I burned through the entire daily budget by 5pm. The restaurant doesn't get busy until seven. So the budget was spent before the window it was meant to influence had even opened.

The cause was keywords that were far too loose. Every click I paid for — all sixteen of them — came from people searching "places to eat near me". Not biryani, not haleem. Just: feed me, anywhere. ₹12.58 a click for traffic with no particular interest in what this restaurant is actually famous for.

I paused it, rebuilt the keyword set, and ran it again.

Decisions and trade-offs

Fixing the Google Business Profile before optimising the site

The instinct is to work on the thing you built. But for a restaurant, the map pack is the surface that gets the "near me" traffic, and the profile feeding it was neglected. Site SEO matters for branded and dish-specific searches; the profile matters for the search that actually happens most.

The trade: profile work is invisible in a portfolio. There's no screenshot that makes it look like craftsmanship.

Judging this on footfall, not on cost per click

Driving CPC from ₹12.58 to ₹2.71 is a real improvement and it's the number that's easy to report. It is also not the point. Clicks aren't customers, and a cheaper click from the wrong person is still the wrong person.

The trade: the honest metric — whether more people walk through the door — is slow, noisy and hard to attribute. I'd rather be judged on it anyway.

Outcome

The site is live, the profile is maintained, and paid search is running on a keyword set that targets what the place is actually known for rather than generic hunger.

I'm not going to claim a revenue result. I don't have reliable attribution from a search click to someone sitting down in an Old City restaurant, and anyone who tells you they do for a business like this is guessing. What I have is a cost per click that came down by a factor of four and a campaign now pointed at the right intent. Whether that turns into footfall is what I'm watching, and it's the number I'd want to be held to.
